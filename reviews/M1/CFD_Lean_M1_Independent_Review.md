**Energy and face orientation.** Write $E_s$ for Blazek’s total energy per mass, $\mathcal E=\rho E_s$ for Lean’s energy per volume, and $m=\rho u$. Blazek’s energy-flux component is $\rho H(u\cdot n)$. His Eq. (2.12) gives $\rho H=\mathcal E+p$. For a uniform state on a planar face of area $A$, taking $S=An$ gives the three integrated components

$$
m\cdot S,\qquad m(u\cdot S)+pS,\qquad(\mathcal E+p)(u\cdot S).
$$

On positive density, $u\cdot S=(m\cdot S)/\rho$, exactly the quantity in `physicalFlux`. The separate reviewer theorem `ReviewerM1.primitiveFlux` establishes componentwise equality between the frozen function on a converted primitive state and this primitive-variable expression in arbitrary finite dimension. It uses the audited pressure-recovery lemma and finite-sum algebra. See `reviewer/PhysicalChecks.lean` and `logs/physical-checks.log`.

**Documented equations and project results.** The books supply the physical state, equation of state, Cartesian or unit-normal flux, and energy convention. Packing a primitive record, extending to arbitrary `Fin d`, using an integrated area vector, and giving total values at degenerate inputs are project choices. The two inverse conversions, equivalence with positive internal energy density, witness of admissibility, additivity in the area vector, scaling, reversal and zero-area identities are project derivations. In particular, a zero function would also satisfy all four area-vector identities. The comparison to the independently written primitive formula and moving-state checks test the content of the flux.

### Assumptions and limiting cases

| Case | Result checked |
|---|---|
| Physical gas and state | The model assumes constant $\gamma=c_p/c_v>1$ and $d\ge1$. The predicates impose $\rho>0$ and $p>0$. For $\gamma>1$, this is equivalent in conservative variables to $\rho>0$ and $\mathcal E>\lVert m\rVert^2/(2\rho)$. Carry the gas assumption alongside the state predicate in downstream contracts. See `State.lean:29–36,84–105` and `specifications/M1.md:7–15`. |
| Inverse conversions | Both directions use the sufficient algebraic hypotheses $\rho\ne0$ and $\gamma\ne1$. The proofs cancel the actual denominators. Positivity is unnecessary for these identities. Converting a conservative admissible state to primitive preserves its two defining inequalities directly. See `State.lean:53–100`. |
| Nonempty domain | In every finite dimension, the primitive state with density 1, zero velocity and pressure 1 produces conservative energy density $1/(\gamma-1)$. It witnesses admissibility for each $\gamma>1$. Dimension zero is a meaningful empty-sum check on the algebra. See `State.lean:102–105`. |
| Zero density or singular gas parameter | Lean defines division by zero, so the raw functions still return values. Zero density violates both physical predicates. At $\gamma=1$, every conservative pressure is zero, so the conservative admissible set is empty. At $\gamma=0$, the raw predicate can accept density 1, zero momentum and energy −1. The reviewer proved these conservative cases explicitly. See `Integrity.lean:17–22` and `reviewer/PhysicalChecks.lean`. |
| Area and flux output | Reversal and zero area hold for the total algebraic flux, including a zero area vector with no unit normal. An oriented flux can have negative mass component and is only using the conservative record’s component layout. See `PhysicalFlux.lean:30–60` and `specifications/M1.md:23–32`. |

The moving 1D state has $\rho=2$, $m=6$, $\mathcal E=14$, $\gamma=2$ and $S=4$. Independent calculation gives $u=3$, $p=5$ and flux $(24,92,228)$. In 2D, $\rho=2$, $u=(3,-1)$, $p=5$, $\gamma=7/5$ and $S=(2,3)$ give $(6,28,9,165/2)$. With the same state and tangential flow relative to $S=(1,3)$, only the momentum pressure contribution survives, giving $(0,5,15,0)$. An exact-rational Python check compared a separately assembled Cartesian tensor contraction with the conservative expression over 9,840 cases in dimensions 0–3. These numerical cases supplement the Lean checks. See `Integrity.lean:12–15` and `logs/exact-arithmetic.log`.

The mathematical scope ends at the exact-real physical Euler flux. Roe linearization and dissipation, mesh assembly, discrete boundary enforcement, time updates, floating-point accuracy and correspondence with compiled SU2 all remain separate claims.
